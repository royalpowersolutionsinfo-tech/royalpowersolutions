What is UPS & Battery Commissioning? Why It Matters for Warranty

October 2026 7 min read Commissioning Division, Royal Power Solutions

When purchasing an Online UPS or a high-capacity battery bank, many facility owners assume that simply connecting the input cables and flipping the power switch is all it takes. In reality, **commissioning** is the single most vital step in your power system's lifecycle.

Without formal commissioning by a certified engineer, minor installation mistakes can cause premature cell failure, unstable inverter output, or even complete cancellation of the manufacturer's warranty.

What Exactly is UPS & Battery Commissioning?

Commissioning is a disciplined engineering process that verifies every component—from input phase synchronisation to battery cell conductance—meets strict factory performance standards before carrying live mission-critical loads.

Key Commissioning Milestones:

  • Chassis & Earth Integrity: Checking neutral-to-earth voltage and earth loop impedance to eliminate ground loops that crash server gear.
  • DC Bus & Battery String Validation: Verifying open-circuit string voltage, inter-cell connector torque, and correct polarity before engaging the DC breaker.
  • Parameter Programming: Setting charge current limits, float/boost voltage thresholds, cut-off voltages, and alarm triggers.
  • Static Bypass & Transfer Testing: Simulating utility grid blackouts to verify seamless 0-millisecond transfer to battery inverter.

Did You Know? Over 80% of UPS battery bank warranty rejections in India occur because the customer cannot produce a certified initial commissioning report showing correct baseline charging voltages.

What is Included in a Commissioning Report?

A legitimate handover report includes individual battery cell voltage logs, ambient temperature readings, static bypass transfer time records, insulation resistance test values, and the engineer's certification stamp—ensuring 100% manufacturer warranty protection.
